This report summarizes the results of the annual supplier risk review. The objective was to identify suppliers that could interrupt operations, increase costs, or create reputational problems over the next twelve months. It's important to note that risk isn't limited to financial failure. Because of geographic concentration, labor issues, cybersecurity weaknesses, or dependence on a single transportation route, a supplier may be stable in financial terms but still present operational risks. And the review considered both direct suppliers and selected sub-tier suppliers. The review used a scoring model that combined spend, criticality, financial health, delivery history, and compliance records. Lots of data sources were considered, including internal purchase records, audit reports, public filings, and supplier questionnaires. Now, the findings suggest that a small number of suppliers account for a disproportionate share of critical risk. In particular, several suppliers provide components for products that cannot be easily switched without a lengthy qualification process. So, even a short disruption could have a significant effect on revenue. Several mitigation options were identified. First, the company should qualify alternate suppliers for the highest-risk components. Second, safety stock levels should be reviewed for items with long lead times. Third, contracts should include clearer requirements for incident notification and continuity planning. And high-risk suppliers should be subject to more frequent audits, especially those operating in regions with elevated regulatory or logistical uncertainty. They may reduce the likelihood of expensive disruptions later. That said, it should be noted that these measures will increase short-term costs. Supplier risk management requires ongoing attention rather than an annual exercise. That current review provides a useful starting point, but the results should be updated as conditions change. And we recommend assigning an owner to each high-risk supplier, setting measurable milestones. And Reporting progress to the operations committee each quarter. Ultimately, the goal isn't to eliminate every risk, which is impossible, but to make the company more prepared and more resilient.
